Decoding Urban Wilderness Perception

How do Chinese and English-speaking publics perceive urban wilderness differently?

Why This Project

Many urban wilderness spaces are discussed online every day, yet planners still rely heavily on surveys and expert interpretation. I wanted to explore whether large-scale public conversations could reveal hidden perception patterns that traditional methods often miss.

Key Findings

01

Recreational over ecological

Public perception of urban wilderness centers on recreational and functional use, not ecological value. Semi-wilderness topics dominated at 60.9%; pure wilderness topics were rare at 3.6%.

02

Culture shapes meaning

Chinese users showed higher Symbolic and Spiritual meaning construction. International users leaned toward Recreational and Functional. Topic type distribution difference was statistically significant (p=0.014).

03

Perception predicts meaning

A multinomial logistic regression model using four perception dimensions achieved 75% accuracy in predicting place meaning type—confirming that how people feel about a space shapes what it means to them.

Reflection

This project changed how I think about design research. Rather than relying on surveys or expert judgment, AI-assisted workflows let me find perception patterns hidden inside thousands of everyday conversations. It was my first experience building a system that transforms unstructured human language into structured design knowledge—and it directly led to asking: what other design workflows could benefit from this kind of structure? That question became PromptDeck.
